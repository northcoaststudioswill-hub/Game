# SOOTBORN — Story bible
Tagline: "Every breath is hers." Current build: v0.12.8 (the surface in real 3D; v0.11 added the Assay, fury, Memory Arts, the film opening and save slots) (see CHANGELOG.md).

## Direction
- Standalone game; not a Pocket Lantern cartridge.
- Spiritually Red Rising, legally distinct (original names, no color castes), so a Steam release stays possible.
- Keep refining the slice: richer maps, lore, characters, grit and polish.
- Cinematics that read clearly; the hero's pain and anger must land; the play itself must feel fun.
- The superiors are glorious, pristine and majestic. We are filth.

## Title
SOOTBORN ("soot" is the Authority's slur; the title reclaims it). A card game called "SootBound" is in development on itch.io, so run a trademark check before selling.

## The crime (playable)
- The clinic was cut to ration air; forty kids were suffocating.
- An Authority-sealed line on Bank Seven carries more air than all of Tier 6. Kael bleeds about 11% of it into the clinic each night and balances it back by dawn.
- Heist, at night in Bay Three:
  1. Cut the audit relay (east wall). This sets a checkpoint.
  2. Reach Bank Seven and solve the "Bleed" puzzle (AUTH line → CLINIC).
- Guards:
  - The Night Proctor walks the middle of the bay end to end and looks up and down at each end. After the relay is cut he walks the north lane past Bank Seven.
  - A second lamp walks the centre column and looks left and right at each end.
  - The Night Watchman stands by the relay and turns all the way round.
  - Their vision cones are drawn on screen; crates and walls block line of sight.
- Lamps sweep smoothly. A detection meter fills while a lamp is on Kael and drains when he breaks line of sight; a full meter sends him back to the checkpoint.
- Cutting the relay is hold-to-work (about two seconds, standing still).
- B throws a bolt: guards within earshot walk over, look all the way around (shown with "?!"), then return to their routes. Seren's distraction helps after three catches.

## Combat
- `src/brawl.js` is the base for all fighting: a pseudo-3D arena. Logic is on a flat plane (x across, y depth); the floor is drawn a scanline at a time from a texture; fighters and props are depth-sorted billboards; `Brawl.cam {x, y, h, f, hor}`.
- Player: move, strike (three-hit chain: cut, reverse cut, leaping overhead), dodge with invulnerability frames.
- Foes telegraph each attack as a red shape on the ground, then commit. Dodging through a committed swing gives an "opening". Foes guard after a full chain.
- **Fury and ELEVEN**: the bar fills on hits given and taken. Full bar, hold A: eleven blows, one for each second the furnace doors stood open. This is the mechanical form of Kael's rage and should stay tied to that image.
- **Memory Arts** (`def.arts`): `{at: hpFraction | on:'wave'|'start', name, line, panel, stage, kind:'rise'|'storm', rgb, hex}`. A story panel and Kael's portrait cut in, the line types out, then the attack plays. Use them to put story beats inside fights: one memory, one sentence, one blow.
- A fight definition can carry `waves`, `allies`, `hero` (sprite, hp, power, blade), `rage` (aura size), `legend` (cannot lose), `doomed` (cannot win), `endAt`, `skip`, `voice` (Kael's narration: timed lines plus `hurt`, `fury`, `eleven`, `wave`, `half`), and `intro` / `outro` shot lists.
- Shots (`Brawl.playCine`): `{d, cam, to, acts, cap, big, bg, sfx, shake, flash, speed, tint}`. Hard cuts, camera moves, captions that advance by themselves. `cu(x, y, s0, s1, dx)` in `src/ff.js` frames a close-up.
- Cutscene step `{brawl: def}` runs a fight inside a cutscene; `{dlg: steps}` runs a dialogue list.

## The Assay (flash-forwards)
- Four years on. The Accredited send their children to a year-long proving war in a valley above the rock: twelve Houses, one winner. Kael, gilded and under a bought name, fights for House Violet.
- It is this game's own invention and uses only our names: the Assay, House Violet, House Teal, Captain Vant, Lucan Aurel (Celestine's son).
- Purpose: show where the rage goes, and give the slow undercity chapters something to fall toward. The two halves should feel like different games: Cinderwell is dark, slow, talk and stealth; the Assay is daylight, fast, almost wordless except for Kael's voice.
- Placement: opening before the title; The Valley at New Game; The Ford between Day 1 and Day 2; The Keep at the end of Day 2.
- Kael's narration is first person, present tense, short. He counts things. He never says he is angry; he describes what he does with it.

### The five fights (v0.12.8)
Only the Valley is a mass battle. The rest are single combat, each with an intro and outro film.
1. **The Valley** (day forty; opens the game) — battle, House Violet against House Teal.
2. **The Pairing** (first night; end of the prologue) — Kael vs Teo Aldane, 16, who shared his bread on the lift. Two sealed in, one comes out a cadet; the loser leaves "by the other door". Teo's last line: "I hope she was worth it." Kael never told him there was a she.
3. **The Ford** (day nineteen; end of Day 1) — single combat with Captain Vant for the crossing.
4. **The Proctor** (day thirty-one; before Last Bell on Day 2) — Proctor Halden Voss has been selling Violet's positions to Teal and comes down to finish them himself. Kael makes a Proctor bleed and wears his white cloak back to camp; the cadets follow Kael, not the House, from that night.
5. **The Keep** (last day; before First Fire) — Lucan Aurel, with Celestine watching.

Rule going forward: above-ground story that is not a fight is told as a film (shot list with captions), never as walk-around play.

## Sound and music
- All audio is synthesised in the browser (`Sound` in `src/core.js`): `tone` (struck), `pad` (swelling), `noise`, `sweep` (moving filtered noise), `ring` (struck steel). No audio files.
- The shift song (`MOTIF`: D F A G | F E D) is the game's theme. It is hummed at the furnace, plays in the home and work tracks, and is the melody of all three Assay battle tracks (`battle`, `dusk`, `duel`). Keep new music on it.
- `Sound.rage` (0 to 1, from the fury bar) drives a shared layer (`TRACKS._rage`) under the Assay tracks.

## Two worlds, two renderers
- Below (Cinderwell, the Ember Ring, the furnace): 2D pixel art on the 320×180 canvas.
- Above (the Assay: valley, ford, keep, and the surface half of the opening): real 3D in `src/g3.js`, a small WebGL renderer with no library. Flat-shaded boxes, cones and cylinders; one sun; planar shadows; fog; additive glow sprites. The pixel HUD, captions and cut-ins are still drawn on the 2D canvas, which sits on top and goes transparent.
- `Brawl` owns the logic and the camera (`cam {x, y, h, f, hor, roll}`); `G3.begin / char / poly / ring / glow / end` draw a frame from it. `G3.proj` gives screen positions back to the 2D layer. `G3.LOOK` holds each place's sky, sun and fog; `G3.env(bg)` builds its scenery once.
- 3D animation is pose to pose: `G3.target()` returns a key pose (joint angles), a spring rate and damping for the fighter's state; `G3.char()` keeps a rig per fighter and springs every joint toward it. Add a move by adding a pose. Attacks are three beats (wind back, cut, hold). Cloth and hair are driven by real velocity.
- Clash: a strike landed in the last 0.17s of an enemy wind-up staggers him.
- Heavy: holding strike for 0.55s (below full fury) fires a guard-breaking launcher. Dash strike: strike during a dodge. Launched fighters without `poise` go to a `floor` state and get up after about a second.
- The style reference is the user's own game Guttercrown (three.js). Three.js itself is not used here because it could not be loaded in the build workspace.

## Art direction
- Reference boards from the user show the target look: dense 16-bit pixel art, a cutaway with a pristine ruling settlement above and lamp-lit warrens below, gold-edged sign plates naming every chamber, banners, and framed menu panels with a world map.
- Those boards use another property's names, emblems and characters. They are mood references only. Nothing from them is copied into the game.
- Translated to this world so far: the cutaway Map of Cinderwell, sign plates, Authority banners (white cloth, gold sun with an eye), and the v0.8 art pass (shaded stone, plate and timber tiles; 16×20 workers and 16×28 Accredited; redrawn props; brass-framed menus).
- Rule of the art: every surface is lit from the top left; the Belowborn world is warm brown and soot; anything of the Authority is white, gold and clean.
- Still short of the reference: it is all drawn by code at a 320×180 screen. Matching the boards fully needs a higher base resolution or hand-made sprite sheets.

## Tech notes
- Cinematic zoom: `Cutscene.zoom` renders a smaller window scaled up, so `W` and `H` are `let`.
- `renderOverlay`: letterbox, flash, heartbeat vignette.
- Cutscene step types include `{zoom}`, `{heart}`, `{vignette}`.
- Heist: `World.throwBolt` / `boltLand` / investigate logic in `updateNPCs`.
- Maps carry `overhead` and `screens` arrays.
- Saves are schema v2; v1 saves migrate. Slots: `auto`, `s1`–`s3` (the old `manual` slot reads as Slot 1). No saving during the prologue, the heist or the festival stealth.
- Dev checkpoints: `Menus.checkpoints()`; captured saves live in `src/checkpoints.js` (regenerate with `DUMP=1 node tests/day2.mjs`).
- `#noff` in the URL skips the opening and flash-forwards (tests use it).
- Day 2 lives in `src/day2.js`: `D2T` (talk) and `D2H` (hotspots) wrap the day-1 tables once `day1Done` is set; `D3` holds epilogue lines.
- `phase()` picks the Pillar color from day flags. Exits take `when` and `deny`.
- Story panels live in `src/panels.js`. A cutscene step `{panel:'id', stage}` cuts to one, `{pstage:n}` advances it, `{panel:null}` cuts back. Keep each panel's focus in the top two thirds: the caption strip covers the bottom.
- Cutscenes: `Cutscene.anim(dt)` runs every frame, including under dialogue. Steps `{kneel}` and `{emote}` give actors simple acting. In landscape the cutscene camera sits low so the action clears the dialogue box.
- Stealth: guards have a facing angle `n.ang` that eases toward their direction; `World.alert` is the detection meter; `World.work` is hold-to-work progress (`WORK_T` seconds).

Shot options used only here: `black`, `credit`, `count`, `bolt`, `sub2`.
