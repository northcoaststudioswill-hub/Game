# SOOTBORN: The Last Day
*A high-energy chapter. The end of the Assay.*

---

**THE ASSAY**
*The last day.*

Dawn comes up the valley the color of a furnace door.

I have been awake since the second watch. I have not moved. I am sitting on the edge of the ridge with my knees drawn up and my white cloak around my shoulders, and below me, in the grey, the whole of the Assay is waking: fires going out, standards coming down, Houses falling into line like columns on a page.

Fifty days. Twelve standards. One keep.

It stands on the far end of the ridge, a block of dressed grey stone with a flat roof and a doorway the height of two men. Banners hang from it, gold and white, snapping in the wind. The Aurel sun with its open eye. A boy stands under the banners with a blade in his hand, perfectly still.

Lucan Aurel. Her son.

I have been looking at him since the light began, the way you look at a gauge you know is about to move.

---

"You have not eaten."

Petra Lune sits down beside me. She has her slate under her arm and her hair pinned flat, and she looks as if she has not slept either. She is eighteen. In a few months she will keep a column of the Count, and she will be good at it, and I will sit two rows behind her and never once say that I remember this.

"One ate last night."

"That was a stone."

"It was bread."

"It was a stone, Sorrel. One watched you." She looks at the keep, not at me. "He is better than you."

"Yes."

"He has had a master since he was six."

"So has everybody." I think of a sealed pump room and a man with a face like weather and a halberd. "I had a better one."

She does not laugh. They do not, very much. But the corner of her mouth moves, and she says nothing, and she puts her slate down on the stone between us and leaves it there.

Behind us the camp has got up. Seventy of them, Violet and Teal, standing in the grey with their blades down. Nobody has given an order. Nobody has to. Oswin has the water. He carried it up the ridge himself, on the bad leg, and put it down by my boots without a word, and I could not tell him what it meant, because he would have had to be told what a tier is, and then why.

I stand.

My knees do not like it. The long legs, the wire, the places where the bones were opened. I have been cold in them for fifty days. But the boots are on, the Proctor's boots, charged on a stack in a storm, and they hum against my ankles like a thing that wants to go.

I look at the dais.

---

It is a white platform on the far slope, a hundred paces from the keep. Wardens stand along the edge of it. Proctors hang above it on their boots, in rows, with their slates. Banners. A canopy. A chair.

She is in the chair.

I have not seen her face in four years. The last time, it was a pale oval at the top of a step, in a bay full of orange light. I would know her anywhere. I would know her by the way she sits, hands folded, back straight, like a woman who has never once had to hold up a thing she was carrying.

She is watching her son.

She does not know my face. She *made* it. Nine months on a table. A throat, a pair of eyes, a head's worth of bone. She sat across a white room from me for four months and never once looked.

*She will have to pin the gold on me herself.*

That is the plan. It has always been the plan. If I put her son on his knees in front of her, in front of every Proctor and every House, she will have to climb out of that chair and walk down the steps and put the sun on my collar with her own hands.

And then I will work for the woman who burned my wife.

I will count who breathes.

I will find the door.

---

"Sorrel."

His voice carries. It is not loud; it just does not need to be. He is standing at the foot of the keep with the sun on his hair, and the banners moving behind him, and the blade in his hand held low and easy, the way a man holds something he has held every day of his life.

"Until the morning," says Lucan Aurel. "It is the morning."

I walk down the ridge.

I do not hurry. I think I know the way, by now. I walk through the gap in the stones with seventy of them behind me, and I stop at the line the Proctors have chalked on the grass, and I put my hand on the hilt of the bank hook, the blade with their steel and our end on it, and I look at him.

"You did not sleep," he says.

"One did not."

"Nor I." He smiles. It is the smile of a boy who has never been told what to do with one. "I should like to say that I do not mind. That the thing will be quick. But I find I am afraid, and I find that I do not mind that either." He lifts the blade. "I have been better than everyone, Sorrel, my whole life. I have never once been afraid. It is quite horrible."

"It gets better."

"Does it?"

"No," I say. "But you learn to count."

He laughs. He actually laughs, and for a moment he looks about twelve. Then the horn goes, a long clean note from the dais that rolls down the valley like a bell, and the laugh is gone, and in its place there is his mother's face.

---

I am too slow.

I know it before it has happened. The first cut comes in out of the sun, a thin bright line, a *question,* and I am not where it lands. I am a hand's width off. It parts the air by my ear. It takes a lock of my hair.

*The first cut is a question.*

The second is the answer.

I am already turning. Already moving. He has a master and a lifetime and a perfect, beautiful, *slow* form, and I do not have any of those things. I have a thing that I was taught in a furnace.

I step *through* the second cut.

*Where were your feet?*

*Here.*

I am inside his guard. The hook is in my hand. I do not know when it came out. I hit him in the ribs with the flat of the hilt, as hard as I have ever hit anything, and he goes backward across the grass with a noise like a bellows, and he stays up.

*Of course he stays up.*

"Good," says Lucan Aurel, with his hand on his side. "That was not in the form."

"Nobody taught it."

"I know." His eyes are very bright. "I should like to learn."

He comes at me.

---

It is the best fight of my life.

I will say that now, because it is true, and because nothing that happens afterward will make it less so. He is *good.* He is quick, and exact, and he has the grace they all have, the long clean lines that come from being raised to it, and he has something none of the rest of them have.

He listens.

Every time I do a thing he has not seen, he stops, a hair of a second, a flicker in the eyes, and he *takes it.* I cut low. He does not know the answer. Two exchanges later, he has it. I go for his legs and he drops a hand to guard them and the second time he does it, he does it before I have moved.

*Quick.* He is so quick. He is going to be better than me by noon.

He cuts. I turn it. He cuts. I turn it. A ring of steel, a ring of steel, and the Houses are making a noise from the slopes like surf, and somewhere above us the Proctors are writing, writing, writing.

Sweat in my eyes.

Something in me goes hot.

*There it is. The thing I keep under the gold.*

It starts low, where the bone was cut. A pressure behind the ribs, a seal about to lift. A bar that fills when I hit and fills when I am hit, and I am being hit, and the bar is going up, up, a tide at the bottom of a drum.

*Not yet. Bank it.*

I try.

He cuts across my ribs and the cloak opens like a wound, and the bar jumps. I feel it jump. I feel a thing in my chest that I know very well, a thing I have not let out since a bay with the doors opening, and a voice at the back of my head that says, in her voice, with the fire on her face: *Be too much.*

*Be too much for THEM.*

---

I fly.

The boots take it. I do not think about it; I do not say a word. I crouch, and I *push,* and the ground drops off me like a dropped coat. The wind takes the cloak and throws it up behind me like a banner. The keep goes under. The stone turns to grey, then to a flat white roof, then to a square of sky, and I am forty feet up with the whole valley under me.

The Houses scream.

The dais is a white coin. The canopy is a white coin. In the middle of it a figure in a chair has moved, only a hair, only the head, to follow me up.

*She sees me.*

I hang there. A breath. Two.

*They came down in light.*

*I went up in it.*

Below me, a small gold figure at the foot of the keep tilts its head back, with the sun on its face and a blade in its hand, and looks at me. And I understand, in that moment, that he is not afraid of the fall. He has never once been afraid of the fall. He does not know how.

*He did not light the furnace.*

I know. *I know.*

I dive.

---

The world goes narrow.

The wind is a scream. The grass is a green wall coming up. The gold figure gets bigger, a hand, an arm, a face, a mouth opening, and I am coming down on it like a shaft dropped down a lift, like a seal letting go, like the whole weight of a tier.

*Shaft drop.*

*ELEVEN.*

It lights.

I feel it light. A white heat from the base of the spine to the roots of my teeth, a sound in the bones like a bell, the whole bar spilling over like a drum run dry. My hands are on the hilt and they are not mine. The hook is bright. The air goes slow.

*ONE.*

I hit his blade. It is a beautiful thing, thin and long and perfectly balanced, and it takes the blow without a quiver.

*TWO.*

He is staggering. He has dropped to one knee. He is looking at me with his mother's eyes, wide and clear and very, very young.

*THREE.*

For the doors.

*FOUR.*

For the washer on her finger, catching the fire.

*FIVE.*

For the tin. For the ash in it, which was less than you would think.

*SIX.*

For a sun painted on a tin lid. The wrong color. *Better.*

*SEVEN.*

For Hollis, who finished the bar he was on.

*EIGHT.*

For the ward, and the forty names in chalk, and a small boy asking for bread.

*NINE.*

For every line struck in a clerk's hand, in a book as wide as a table.

*TEN.*

For every one who knelt. For every one who watched. For every one who did not hum.

I raise it for the last.

I could kill him.

It is simple. It is very simple. A boy on his knees with a broken guard and his throat bare, and a blade in my hand that has been waiting fifty days. I could do it. Nobody would stop me. Nobody would even write it down.

*That is not what I came up here for.*

*ELEVEN.*

I bring it down on his blade, not on him. On the thin bright steel in his two raised hands, with every one of the ten before it behind it like a wall.

It breaks.

---

The sound is very small.

I had thought it would be louder. It is a note, high and clean, like a glass tapped with a spoon. The blade goes in two. The tip spins away across the grass, flashing, and lands point-down in the earth like a candle.

The valley goes silent.

The wind drops.

Lucan Aurel kneels in the grass with half a sword in his hands and the sun in his hair, in front of a keep with his mother's banners on it, in front of a dais a hundred paces away. He does not move. He looks at the broken blade as if it were a word he did not know. He looks up at me.

"I am not dead," he says.

"No."

"Why?"

I do not have an answer I can say aloud. I think of a bench in a square and a pair of hands, and a girl who held a belt, and a woman who walked in without being pushed. I think: *she would have bitten you.*

"One does not," I say. "It is not what one came for."

He looks at me for a long time. His mouth moves a little at the corner. It is not the smile from before. It is something smaller and much older, and I do not think he has ever worn it before.

"I find," says Lucan Aurel, "that I do not mind as much as I was supposed to."

He puts what is left of the blade down on the grass in front of him, very carefully, as if it might wake somebody.

---

I turn round.

Nobody is moving. Not the Houses. Not the Wardens at the edge of the dais. Not the Proctors on their boots, slates frozen, pens lifted. Seventy of mine stand on the ridge with their blades hanging, and none of them has made a sound.

I look at the dais.

She has risen.

I did not see her do it. One moment she was in the chair, with her hands in her lap and her face a pale oval; the next she is on her feet, at the top of the steps, with the banners moving behind her and her gloves very white against the white of her dress. She is looking at her son. She is looking at the broken blade, and the boy kneeling, and at me.

Her face does not change.

I have watched that face for four years in the dark behind my eyes. I know it the way I know a gauge. I know the way it goes empty, the way it goes smooth, the way it tilts a quarter-turn when somebody does something she did not expect.

It tilts.

She comes down one step.

And then, slowly, as if it were a thing she had not quite meant to do, High Auditor Celestine Aurel leans forward.

I have seen that look.

I have seen it in a square, over a kneeling crowd, on a morning when a girl stood up out of the front row with her chin raised and said *it was me.* I have seen it in a bay full of orange light, on a raised step, with her hands folded and her head a little to one side, as a door closed.

She is *interested.*

Behind me a wind comes up from the valley, and the banners on the keep lift all together, gold and white, like a great hand opening.

I am standing on the grass with a broken sword at my feet and the gold of her son's hair in the corner of my eye, and I think, very clearly, in the last quiet second before it all begins:

*There you are.*
