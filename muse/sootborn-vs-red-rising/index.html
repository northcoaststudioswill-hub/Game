Story comparison · Sootborn v0.84, story complete

SootbornvsRed Rising

The same skeleton with different organs. Sootborn keeps Red Rising's spine (a dead wife, a man remade into a master, a rigged trial, a climb to the top) and then spends its time on the things Red Rising moves past quickly: grief, ordinary life below, and the arithmetic of who gets to breathe.

03 · People

Who stands in for whom

Most of Sootborn's cast splits one Red Rising role into two people or folds two into one. The bar is the same one-to-five closeness score.

In Red Rising, missing from Sootborn

  • A Sevro: the feral best friend at the hero's side through everything.
  • A Mustang romance. Kestrel Dray is the Accredited who asks why, with no love story.
  • A pack like the Howlers. Kael's cadets follow him, but no named band grows up around him.
  • Space. No fleets between planets; the sky is one valley and one tower.

In Sootborn, not in Red Rising

  • Celestine's secret: the ruler was born on Tier Six and won her own trial by shutting a valve.
  • Seren found the bleed first and chose to answer for it, played as her in Bay Three.
  • The Count as a puzzle: Kael rebalances the book and writes his own name in black.
  • A choice at the end: strike her line, or send her home to Row K.
  • Playable side people: Tavi, Mara, Brand, Seren and Celestine each get a level.

07 · Inverse characters

09 · Voices, measured

The speech rules hold in the script

The story bible sets the rules: soot use contractions, "aye" and "we"; the Accredited never contract and say "one". I pulled 1,008 spoken lines out of the game's source on the sootborn branch and counted. The rule is kept almost perfectly: every Accredited speaker is at zero contractions.

contractions per 100 words (soot speech)"one" as a pronoun, per 100 words (Accredited speech)

Vale slips, as writtenThe bible says Vale "speaks their way, with contractions slipping". The script has him at 1.2 contractions per 100 words, the only speaker between the two camps.

Brand speaks like neitherNo contractions, and never "one". A Warden who left the Authority but never learned to talk like the people below.

Celestine barely says "one"At 0.6 she uses it far less than the cadets (3 to 4.5). She doesn't need the grammar to prove what she is, which fits a woman born on Tier Six.

Two speakers whose grammar changes

Kael below counts his lines in Part One and the walkable memories; as Sorrel, the Assay and the Spire up to the speech before the Stair; after, from "It was always we" on. Kestrel early is her lines at the Assay and the Ledger; late is Part Two. Small samples: 16 to 114 lines each.

Red Rising marks its castes mostly with vocabulary: invented curses, Latin mottos, each colour's slang. Sootborn marks them with grammar, and then makes the grammar move. That is also the safest kind of difference for a story that cannot borrow Red Rising's words.

13 · Deeper reading

What the comparison shows

The villain is a sum

Red Rising's enemies are people with appetites: the Jackal's cruelty, the Sovereign's grip on power. Sootborn's enemy is a piece of arithmetic that several people say aloud. "Balanced" appears 41 times in the script. Corin sells his House and calls it "only arithmetic". Voss, beaten, says "It does not... balance." Maren signs her betrayal with it. Celestine is the final fight because she is the sum's best practitioner, not its author.

One for forty. I did that sum at twenty, with two cadets who did not wake. I have done it every day since.

Kael is complicit, on the page

Darrow spends years living as a Gold, and the books worry about what it does to him. Sootborn puts a number on it: as Clerk of the Count, Kael has struck 406 lines himself. When he saves forty people at Riser Nine, he writes "clerical" beside the difference in his own hand. The hero who hates the book is the book's best clerk, and the game lets you see it.

The order said eleven hundred. I was back at my desk before the bell, and I wrote "clerical" beside the difference.

Seren chose first

Eo's death is a message aimed at Darrow. Seren's turns out to be her own decision about her own work: she traced the bleed and asked Orin about it weeks before Kael touched the bank. The game holds that back until Part Two and has you play it as her. Her last words are a work order: "Balance the bank."

Somebody will have to answer for it. Let it be somebody who knew.

Rage is turned into counting

Red Rising's hero becomes "the Reaper", and his rage is the engine. Kael's rage has a meter (fury, then ELEVEN), and the bible's rule is that he never says he is angry, only what he does with it. By the end he isn't counting seconds any more; he is counting everyone else.

I have counted eleven seconds every day for nine years. Mara has counted forty-four commitments.

The cost is inside the ending

Red Rising wins its revolution in book three, and the cost is the subject of the books that follow. Sootborn puts the cost into its own last pictures: the Array does not hold, and the dead of the first winter are read aloud by name. Then it hands the player the choice of what to do with the woman who ran the sum.

Eat it while it's hot, little ember. Nobody's counting your bowl.

Everyone is offered the same deal

Celestine makes the same offer again and again: a post, a better name, and a column to keep, in exchange for yourself. Kael takes it and stays himself. Oswin takes it and is masked. Ilse refused one line of it and was struck. Maren keeps the book either way. Red Rising has one tempter at a time; Sootborn has one offer and makes everyone answer it.

One had another name. She took it when she gave one the post.

Craft notes: what Red Rising does that Sootborn could use

  • A friend at his side above. Darrow always has Sevro. Kael's friend Ivo has 36 lines, nearly all below, with one on the barges the night before the Fall. The script plan says "Nothing of his own". One real scene for Ivo in Part Two would give Kael someone who knew him before.
  • Villains with blood in it. The Bellona feud works because it is family against family. The script plan calls Severin and Marshal Cade "the thinnest villains in the game": four lines between them in the dialogue pulled here. One reason for the Marshal's balancing would help.
  • Already stronger than Red Rising: the mirrored cast, the speech system, eleven as a single device across story, combat and editing, and an antagonist the hero could become.
  • Keep the out-of-order telling readable. 58 cards and 25 jumps of a year or more is a lot. The cards and transition rules in the master timeline carry it; the calendar strip should stay on every Assay flash.
