# Starhaven — World Bible: The Fallen Sky

Chosen by Will on Nov 28, 2026, after the playtest review said the story mirrored its source novels too closely.
This replaces the fae-court setting. **The plot's shape stays; the world it happens in changes.** Where this file and
DESIGN.md or STORY.md disagree about the world, this file wins. Nothing in the game has been changed yet.

Working subtitle: **Starhaven: The Fallen Sky** (the old "A Court of Dusk and Starlight" echoes the books' titles).

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

The sky is a country. Its people, the **Lit**, are born from stars, and they live in seven **Houses**, each built
around a constellation. Mortals live **Below**, under the sky, and the line between the two is the **Horizon**: a
curtain of light at the edge of the world that only opens at dawn. Mortals poach **low stars**, the small ones that
drift down near the treetops in winter, and sell their light to lamp merchants. Wren is a mortal who shoots the wrong
one.

## 2. The spine (unchanged from the current story)

1. A poor huntress kills something sacred to feed her sisters.
2. She is taken across a border as payment, into a beautiful prison with a kind but controlling captor.
3. A tyrant seizes that world, and she goes in after her captor.
4. She wins the tyrant's trials, dies, and is brought back by a rival lord, now bound to him.
5. He takes her to his hidden city, where she finds a family.
6. She carries power stolen from every House.
7. Her old captor sides with a foreign conqueror to get her back, and her sisters become the target.
8. The siege, and her crowning.

## 5. People

Names and personalities stay. What changes is where they come from.

- **Wren**: mortal, a hunter and painter, Below. After the Umbra she is Lit, with Caelum's light in her and the
  stolen sparks of every House. Her freckles glow.
- **Nessa and Lissa**: her sisters, still Below. Unchanged.
- **Lord Rowan of the Dawn House**: takes Wren up the Stair. No antlers; a crown of morning rays.
- **Vael, the Eclipse**: the tyrant. Gone after the prologue, but her black glass is everywhere, and so is she in
  Wren's dreams.
- **Lord Caelum of the Dusk House**, ruler of Starhaven. Tethered to Wren by his own choice. Dimmer than he was.
- **The Five**: **Bram** (general), **Sable** (spymaster), **Iris** (seer: she reads the sky, literally, as an
  astrologer reads stars), **Mira** (diplomat, Caelum's cousin), **Thane** (keeper of the Library and its star charts).
- **The Iron King**: Below, across the sea. Mines fallen stars.
- **The Cartographer**: draws the sky.

## 8. The Tether, and the romance

This is the biggest story change and the reason this world works better than the old one.

- In the books, and in the current game, the bond is fate: he felt it first, she only had to notice it. There is
  nothing to push against, and the review said so ("a romance with no wrong answers").
- Here, Caelum **made a choice for her**. He tied his light to hers while she was dead. It saved her life, and it
  means she will feel him for the rest of it whether she wants to or not.
- So Wren has a real grievance, and Caelum has something to answer for. Some choices now cost him bond points:
  forgiving him too easily, or never asking. The slow burn is her deciding, over chapters, whether to keep the Tether
  or have Thane find a way to cut it. That decision can be the romance ending in Chapter 9.
