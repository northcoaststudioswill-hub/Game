# Romantasy Court Game — Design Outline

Nov 16, 2026 · @William Cohen

## The pitch

A mortal huntress, now reborn as fae, helps run a hidden city of starlight while a foreign king prepares to invade. You split your time between three things: rebuilding the city, getting closer to the High Lord and his inner circle, and going out on action missions against monsters and spies. Each one makes the others stronger, and the story builds toward a siege of the city you've been building.

**Format: a 2D side-scrolling action RPG for Steam and mobile, played in landscape.** Characters are crisp pixel art moving in front of layered, hand-painted fantasy backgrounds. Think **Stardew Valley's home base + Fire Emblem's relationship talks + side-scrolling action missions**, with Hollow Knight's moody, atmospheric look. It's inspired by the middle of *A Court of Thorns and Roses* (books 2–3).

## Which slice of the story

**Recommendation: the Night Court era (books 2–3).** This is the stretch you described: she's married into power, helping run a city, building relationships, and getting ready for a war. Book 1 is mostly a slow romance followed by a dungeon of trials, so it works better as a flashback prologue than as the main game.

| Slice | Story it covers | What kind of game it makes | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Under the Mountain (book 1 ending) | A captive human facing a tyrant queen's three deadly trials, helped by a mysterious High Lord through a magical bargain | A dark puzzle and trial dungeon. Short and intense, with no city and little relationship-building | Good as a prologue |
| **B. The city and the coming war (books 2–3)** | Recovering from the trials, joining the Night Court's inner circle, the hidden city, the romance, and preparing for invasion | A city hub, a cast of companions, missions, and a siege finale | **Best fit** |
| C. The war itself (book 3 ending) | Armies, alliances between courts, battles | Strategy or battle-heavy. Hard to make feel good on a phone | Use as the finale only |

**Where the books stand:** the Hulu TV series (not an anime) was dropped in February 2025, and Maas has since gotten the adaptation rights back. Book 6 came out October 27, 2026, and book 7 is due January 12, 2027 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/sarah-j-maas-a-court-of-thorns-and-roses-tv-adaptation-hulu/), [TV Insider](https://www.tvinsider.com/swooon/1157042/acotar-tv-show-updates-cast-premiere-date-news/)). Slice B is safe ground: it's from the first three books, and you don't need the new ones to follow it.

## Making it ours

The characters, places and story are our own, with their own names and designs. Since this is headed for Steam and sale, the plot should become its own story too. Keep the feel of the books (fae courts, a slow-burn romance, a looming war), but avoid retelling their specific events. I can't draw Maas's characters, and staying original also protects a paid release. I'm not a lawyer, so get real legal advice before launch. The working names below are placeholders you can change.

| In the books | In our game (working name) | Role in the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feyre | **Wren**, a huntress and painter | You. She fights with a bow and daggers, and paints murals to bring the city back to life |
| Rhysand / the Night Court | **Lord Caelum**, High Lord of the Court of Dusk | Main romance and mentor, and the ruler you co-lead with |
| Velaris | **Starhaven**, the hidden city | The home base you rebuild |
| The inner circle | **The Five**: a warrior, a spymaster, a seer, a cousin who's a diplomat, and a scholar | Companions with their own bond stories and abilities |
| Hybern's king | **The Iron King** from across the sea | The main villain, mostly unseen until the finale |
| Amarantha / Under the Mountain | **The Thorn Queen** and **the Deep** | The prologue and Wren's nightmares |

Wren's sisters stay in the story too. Rescuing and protecting them is the emotional core of the middle chapters, just as it is in the books.

## Core loop

&#91;embedded content: Core loop: missions, city, bonds, story\]

Missions earn Starlight to rebuild the city. The city gives you gear and places to spend evenings with companions. Stronger bonds bring allies and combo moves into missions. The next chapter only opens once all three have moved forward.

## The four modes

### 1. Starhaven: the city

The city is your home base. It starts dim and half-empty, and you bring it back one district at a time. It's one big side-scrolling city on three levels (riverside, streets, rooftops), linked by stairs and wind-lifts, with fast-travel lanterns between districts.

- **Districts to restore:** the Rainbow Quarter (artists' quarter, unlocks mural painting), Night Market (shop and trading), Healers' Hall (potions and revives), Training Ring (combat upgrades), the Library (lore and spells), and the Rooftop House (your home: decorate it, and it's where bond scenes happen).
- **Residents** post small requests on a board, like "find my lost cat in the market" or "bring river-lilies." They're quick, low-stakes errands.
- **Mural painting** is Wren's signature move. It's a simple trace-and-fill minigame: each mural you finish lights up a street and raises the city's Prosperity.

### 2. Bonds: the people

This works like Fire Emblem support conversations or Persona social links.

- Each of the Five plus Caelum has **5 bond hearts**. You raise them by spending evenings together, giving gifts they like, and taking them on missions.
- Each heart unlocks a short scene, and some hearts unlock a **combo move** for missions.
- Caelum's track is the romance. It keeps the books' banter, slow burn and devotion, and fades to black where the books get spicy.
- **The sisters** have their own track. Rebuilding trust with them unlocks the chapter where Wren protects them.

### 3. Missions: the action

Missions are side-scrolling levels about 5–10 minutes long, split into sections with checkpoints so they still fit a short mobile session.

- **Wren's kit:** jump (with a double jump later), daggers (tap combo, including an air combo), a bow (aim in 8 directions), and a dodge-dash.
- **Court gifts:** in the books she's reborn with a piece of each High Lord's power. Here those are 7 unlockable powers: flame, frost, shadow, light, tide, thorn and wind. You equip 2 at a time, and each also opens new paths, Metroidvania-style.
- **Enemies:** forest wyrms, winged spies, iron-masked soldiers, bog things and a few boss monsters. **No enemies until the controls are taught.**
- **Mission types:** monster hunts, stealth infiltration, escort (getting the sisters out), climbing and chase levels, and defense.

### 4. Story chapters

Story scenes play out as cutscenes with character portraits at key moments: arrivals, betrayals, big reveals. Finishing a chapter's requirements in the other three modes unlocks the next chapter, so the story moves at your pace.

## Controls and platforms

The game is landscape on every platform. It's built controller-first and adapted to touch, so the Steam and mobile versions play the same.

| Action | Controller (Steam, Steam Deck) | Keyboard | Touch (landscape phone) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move | Left stick / D-pad | A / D | Floating stick, left thumb |
| Jump | A | Space | Big button, right thumb |
| Daggers | X | J | Attack button |
| Bow (hold to aim) | Hold Y + left stick | Hold K + aim with mouse | Hold the bow button, drag to aim |
| Dodge-dash | B | Shift | Swipe or dash button |
| Court gifts (2 slots) | LB / RB | Q / E | Two small buttons above attack |
| Talk, interact | A near a person | W | Tap the speech bubble |
| Menu | Start | Esc | Pause icon, top corner |

- **Touch comfort:** buttons can be moved and resized, and an optional **auto-aim** for the bow helps on phones.
- **Text:** large and readable on a phone held sideways, with subtitles on every voiced or cutscene line.
- **Saves:** short sessions with autosave after every mission, evening or mural, plus Steam Cloud and mobile cloud saves.

## Progression and rewards

There are four progress tracks, each tied to a different mode, so every session moves something forward.

| Track | Grows from | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wren's level | Missions | Health, new gift slots, and upgrades for each court gift |
| City Prosperity | Murals, restored districts, resident requests | New shops, better gear, more residents, and city defenses for the siege |
| Bond hearts | Evenings, gifts, missions together | Scenes, combo moves, and companions who join missions |
| Story chapter | Each chapter's goals across all three modes | The next chapter's cutscenes and areas |

- **Currencies:** *Starlight* comes from missions and requests and goes toward building. *Mural paint* comes from the market and hunts.
- **Starfall wishes:** a gacha-style pull for outfits, house decor and color palettes for Wren's murals. It's paid for only with in-game Starlight, so it's cosmetic only.
- **Achievements journal:** styled as Wren's sketchbook. Each achievement fills in a page with a drawing.
- **Daily evening:** one free bond evening per real day, plus a small Starlight bonus.

## Story outline

The story runs nine chapters. It goes from a broken, newly reborn Wren to the High Lady who defends her city. Chapters follow the books' arc loosely, with our own names and details.

| # | Chapter | What happens | How it plays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Prologue: The Deep | A playable nightmare of the Thorn Queen's trials, and of dying and being reborn fae | A short dark dungeon with a riddle, a maze and a beast. It teaches movement only |
| 1 | Ashes | Wren is trapped in a gilded life at another court. Caelum calls in their old bargain and brings her to Starhaven | Safe tutorial: walking, talking, the first mural. No enemies |
| 2 | The Five | Wren meets the inner circle, who are rough, funny and loyal | Bond intros. The Training Ring teaches the bow, daggers and dodge on targets, then the first real enemies |
| 3 | First Light | Wren discovers she carries a spark of every High Lord's power | Unlocks court gifts (flame first). A monster hunt in the mountains |
| 4 | The Spy in the Library | Winged spies attack the Library | Defense mission inside the city, so the city now matters in a fight |
| 5 | The Weaver's Cottage | Stealing a relic from an ancient, terrifying witch | Stealth level where getting seen means running |
| 6 | Starfall | A night of celebration under falling stars, and Wren realizes she's in love | No combat. A festival, a bond climax and the romance confession |
| 7 | The Sisters | The Iron King takes Wren's mortal sisters and changes them | Escort and rescue mission. Bonds with the sisters begin |
| 8 | Siege of Starhaven | The Iron King's army hits the city | Finale: defend each district you restored. Your companions and city upgrades all count |
| 9 | High Lady | Wren is crowned co-ruler of the Court of Dusk | An ending scene, then free play and the post-game |

## Art and audio

The look is **pixel-art characters in front of hand-painted fantasy backgrounds**. Characters stay sharp and readable, and the world behind them feels lush and storybook.

- **Sprites:** characters about 48–64 px tall, drawn at a native 640×360 game resolution and scaled up cleanly to 1080p, 1440p and 4K. That's 16:9, so it fits Steam, Steam Deck and landscape phones. Animation priorities: run, jump, dagger combo, bow draw, dash, hurt, and idle breathing.
- **Backgrounds:** painted in 4–6 parallax layers: a far sky (stars, aurora), distant mountains or city silhouette, the mid-ground city, the play layer, and a blurred foreground of leaves, lanterns and banners. Painted at higher resolution than the sprites for the contrast.
- **Palette by place:** Starhaven in night blues, violets and starlight gold. Prologue and Spring-style memories in pale greens and roses. The mountains in cold slate and snow. The Iron King's army in rust, black and sickly green. You can always tell where you are by color.
- **Lighting:** dynamic glow on lanterns, spells and murals. The city visibly brightens as Prosperity rises.
- **Fantasy influences:** fae courts, winged warriors, starlit rivers, ancient witches' cottages, and mountain palaces carved into rock. Original designs throughout.
- **Cutscenes:** illustrated painted panels with pixel portraits for dialogue, used at big story beats.
- **Music:** your own tracks, credited, with themes for the city at night, missions, bond scenes and the siege. Separate music and sound-effect volume sliders.

## Platforms and tech

Target platforms are Steam (Windows, Mac, Steam Deck), then iOS and Android, all in landscape.

- **Engine:** Godot 4 is my recommendation. It's free, strong for 2D pixel art, and one project exports to PC, Mac, Linux, iOS and Android. Steam features (achievements, cloud saves) are added through a plugin.
- **Prototype first:** the first milestone is a movement test room built in Godot itself (see the base build section below).
- **Store costs:** Steam charges a one-time $100 Steam Direct fee per game, which it gives back once the game earns $1,000 ([Presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-much-does-steam-take)). Mobile stores have their own developer account fees.
- **Monetization:** a one-time purchase on Steam. On mobile, either the same paid app or a free demo with a one-time unlock. No paid gacha: Starfall wishes stay earned-only.

## Visual influence: Hollow Knight

Hollow Knight is the reference for **mood and atmosphere only**. The gameplay stays as designed above: the city hub, bonds, and missions picked from the map.

| What we borrow | How it looks in our game |
| --- | --- |
| Deep parallax with fog between layers | Mist drifting between the painted mountain, city and foreground layers, so every scene feels deep |
| Moody, limited palettes per area | Each region gets 3–4 main colors plus one glowing accent (starlight gold in Starhaven, ember orange in the Iron King's camps) |
| Strong, readable silhouettes | Wren and the enemies have clear outlines and a rim light, so they stand out against busy painted backgrounds |
| Light as the focal point | Lanterns, spells, murals and starlight are the brightest things on screen and lead the eye |
| Quiet, melancholy beauty | Overgrown ruins, empty streets and still water in the wilds, with long pauses and no clutter |
| Drifting particles | Floating starlight motes, falling petals in the forest, ash near the front |
| Minimal HUD | Just hearts (small star-gems) and gift pips (moon phases) in a corner. Everything else stays off-screen |

**What stays ours:** pixel-art characters rather than hand-drawn ones, and a warmer, more romantic palette. The wilds are dark and atmospheric like Hollow Knight, but Starhaven gets warmer and brighter as you restore it. That contrast is part of the story. We borrow the feeling, never Hollow Knight's actual designs or characters.

## Base build: keeping token use low

The plan is set up so each build session is small, focused and cheap.

- **Art comes from outside, code comes from me.** Generating pixel art or painted backgrounds in code costs a lot of tokens and looks worse than real art. Use placeholder shapes until art arrives from an asset pack, a pixel artist, or your own painting.
- **Data, not code, for content.** Enemies, gifts, sigils, districts, companions and dialogue live in small data files. Adding a new enemy or a line of dialogue means editing one row, not rewriting scripts.
- **Small files, one job each:** player, enemy base, gift, shrine, save, dialogue. A change touches one short file, and I edit that piece instead of reprinting whole files.
- **One reusable enemy and one room template.** Each new enemy is the base enemy plus a few settings, and each room starts from the same template.
- **Go straight to Godot.** A separate browser prototype would mean building the movement twice. You run each build on your machine and send short notes back ("jump feels floaty", "dash too short"), with screenshots only when it's something visual.
- **This doc is the source of truth.** Start each session by naming the section you're building ("Combat system, daggers + dash") so nothing needs to be re-explained.
- **One milestone per session:**
  1. Movement feel: run, jump, double jump, dash in an empty test room
  2. Daggers, one enemy (thorn sprite), getting hit, health
  3. One short mission level with checkpoints, plus saving and loading
  4. Starhaven hub stub with one door per activity
  5. Dialogue box and one bond evening with Caelum
  6. First court gift (Flame) and a thorn wall it burns away

After milestone 6, the core of the game is real and every later piece adds to it rather than reworking it.

## Combat system

Combat is light and readable: simple hits, generous dodges, and no stat math the player has to think about. The numbers below are starting values to tune in playtests.

| Stat | Start | Max | How it grows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health (hearts) | 5 | 12 | +1 every 2 levels, plus Healers' Hall upgrades |
| Dagger damage | 2 | 8 | Training Ring upgrades |
| Bow damage (full charge) | 4 | 14 | Training Ring upgrades. Half charge does half damage |
| Gift power (mana) | 3 pips | 8 pips | +1 at levels 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15 |
| Level cap | 1 | 20 | XP from missions only |

- **Daggers:** a 3-hit ground combo, or a 2-hit air combo. The third hit knocks enemies back.
- **Bow:** hold the bow button to draw (0.6 s to full charge) and aim in 8 directions, and you walk slowly while aiming. Arrows are unlimited.
- **Dodge:** a short dash (works in the air too) with about 0.3 s of invincibility, then a 0.5 s cooldown. A perfect dodge (dashing right as you're hit) slows time for 1 s.
- **Gifts:** tap a gift button to cast. Each cast costs 1–3 pips, and pips refill slowly over time or quickly from dagger hits, which rewards getting close.
- **Getting knocked out:** you restart at the last checkpoint with no penalty except time. This is meant to be chill, not punishing.
- **Difficulty:** one setting at launch, tuned easy. A "Story" toggle halves enemy damage.

## Court gifts

Wren carries a spark from each of the seven High Lords. She can equip two gifts at a time, and each one also has a use outside combat that opens new paths in levels, so earlier areas are worth revisiting.

| Gift | Unlocks | In combat | Outside combat | Upgrade (Library) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flame | Ch 3 | Fireball that burns over time | Light braziers, burn away thorn walls | Bigger blast, then a fire trail on dodge |
| Shadow | Ch 4 | Short blink-teleport through enemies | Hide in shadows during stealth | Longer range, then a stun on arrival |
| Frost | Ch 5 | Freeze ray that slows, then freezes | Freeze water into walkways | Longer freeze, then frozen enemies shatter |
| Light | Ch 6 | Flash that blinds everything nearby | Reveal hidden paths and murals | Wider flash, then it heals 1 heart |
| Thorn | Ch 7 | Root enemies in place with vines | Grow vine ladders | Roots deal damage, then the root area spreads |
| Tide | Ch 7 | Water wave that pushes enemies back | Fill or drain pools | Wider wave, then it knocks enemies down |
| Wind | Ch 8 | Updraft for a short glide over gaps | Reach rooftops and ledges | Longer glide, then a dive attack |

The last upgrade on each gift is the **mastery** tier. It costs a rare Starlight Shard, earned from bosses, from a companion's fifth bond heart, and from post-game hunts.

## Starhaven districts

Each district has 3 restoration stages. Every stage costs Starlight plus one finished mural, so painting and building advance together. In the siege, each restored district gives you a defense.

| District | Opens | Stage 1 → 3 cost (Starlight) | What it gives | Siege role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rooftop House | Ch 1 | Free → 200 → 500 | Home, bond evenings, decor | Command post, where you choose companions |
| Rainbow Quarter | Ch 1 | 100 → 300 → 700 | Mural studio, new paint colors | Glowing murals reveal invisible attackers |
| Training Ring | Ch 2 | 150 → 400 → 900 | Dagger and bow upgrades | Trained guards hold one street |
| Night Market | Ch 3 | 200 → 500 → 1,000 | Shop: potions, gifts, decor, paint | Supply runs that refill health mid-siege |
| Library | Ch 4 | 250 → 600 → 1,200 | Gift upgrades, lore journal | Ward spells slow the brutes |
| Healers' Hall | Ch 6 | 300 → 700 → 1,400 | Max hearts, revive charm | One free revive in the finale |

Restoring all 6 districts to stage 3 earns the "City of Starlight" achievement and the true ending scene.

## The cast

Eight companions to bond with. Each has a liked gift, a perk when you bring them on a mission, and a combo move unlocked at heart 3. Names are working names.

| Character | Who they are | Loves (gift) | Mission perk | Combo move (heart 3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **Bram** | General and brawler, the big brother of the group | Spiced bread, sparring | Takes 1 hit for Wren per mission | **Shieldwall:** charges ahead and knocks enemies flat |
| **Sable** | Spymaster, quiet and deadly | Throwing knives, silence | Stealth: guards notice you 30% slower | **Twin Shadows:** a linked blink strike |
| **Iris** | Seer who sees threads of fate, oddly cheerful | Old maps, sugared plums | Shows hidden chests on the map | **Fate Thread:** your next 3 hits are critical |
| **Mira** | Diplomat and Caelum's cousin, a fearless flirt | Silk ribbons, gossip | Shops give 15% off | **Dazzle:** charms 2 enemies into fighting for you |

**Bond pacing:** heart 1 comes free when you meet. Hearts 2–5 need bond points (an evening gives +20, a liked gift +15, a mission together +10). Hearts take 40, 80, 120 and 160 points.

## Economy

The target pace: in a 15-minute session the player should be able to afford one meaningful thing, like a district stage, a gift upgrade or a nice gift.

| Currency | Earned from | Spent on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starlight** (main) | Missions (60–150), resident requests (20–40), daily evening bonus (25) | District stages, Night Market items, Starfall wishes |
| **Mural paint** | Hunts (enemy drops), Night Market, the rooftop garden | Murals: 3–6 paint each |
| **Gift essence** | Mission chests, bosses | Gift upgrades at the Library (tiers 1–2) |
| **Starlight Shard** (rare) | Bosses, a companion's fifth heart, post-game hunts | Mastery tier for a gift |
| **XP** | Missions only | Wren's level |

- **Starfall wish:** 100 Starlight per pull. Each pull gives one cosmetic (outfit, decor, mural palette or a portrait frame). There are no duplicates until you own a full set, so a pull is never wasted.
- **Pace check:** the full campaign should take about 8–10 hours. Everything except the post-game should be reachable without grinding.
